★ Kenny's #1 Insight — TWO WAYS to solve, both correct
Try this: $\;4\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{6}} - \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{10}}\;$ → final answer is $\dfrac{17\sqrt{30}}{30}$. You can get there two completely different ways:
WAY 1 — Rationalize each term FIRST, then combine
$\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{6}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{10}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{\sqrt{10}}$ $\;= \dfrac{4\sqrt{30}}{6} - \dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{10} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{30}}{3} - \dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{10}$ LCD 30: $\;\dfrac{20\sqrt{30} - 3\sqrt{30}}{30} = \dfrac{17\sqrt{30}}{30}$
WAY 2 — Build a common $\sqrt{30}$ denominator FIRST, rationalize at the end
$\dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{6}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{10}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}$ $\;= \dfrac{20}{\sqrt{30}} - \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{30}} = \dfrac{17}{\sqrt{30}}$ $\;= \dfrac{17}{\sqrt{30}} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{\sqrt{30}} = \dfrac{17\sqrt{30}}{30}$
Whichever feels easier for the problem in front of you is the right method. Pick a side, but know both.
