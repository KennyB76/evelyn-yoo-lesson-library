Bhaskara's Proof — Step by Step

1 Start with a big square of side $(a + b)$. Its total area is $(a + b)^2$.
2 Layout A — place 4 copies of our right triangle in the corners, all pointing inward, hypotenuse facing the center. The leftover space in the middle is a tilted square of side $c$, with area $c^2$. So $(a+b)^2 = c^2 + 4 \cdot \frac{ab}{2}$.
3 Layout B — rearrange the same 4 triangles into two corners. The leftover space is now two squares: one of area $a^2$, one of area $b^2$. So $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 4 \cdot \frac{ab}{2}$.
4 Both layouts fit inside the same $(a+b)^2$ square and use the same 4 triangles. So the leftover spaces are equal: $\;\;a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. ✓
