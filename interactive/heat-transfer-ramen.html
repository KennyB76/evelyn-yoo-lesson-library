Heat Transfer — Cooking Ramen

Three ways heat moves, all happening in one pot of ramen.
Today's idea: Heat doesn't just “exist” — it travels. And it has three different ways of getting from place to place. The cool part? All three are happening, right now, while you cook ramen. Watch the chopstick. Watch the soup. Watch the sunny window. Three transit systems, one bowl.

Conduction Heat travels through the chopstick itself — atom bumping atom, like a row of dominoes. Metal atoms pass the signal fast; plastic atoms barely pass it at all. That’s conduction — touch-to-touch heat transfer.
Think Why does Mr. Kenny tell you not to leave a metal spoon sitting in the pot?
Module 2Convection — The Soup Currents
Hot rises, cold sinks, and the water moves in a loop.
Flame Control

0 = off, 1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high. Higher = faster, wilder loop.
Watch what happens to the loop when the flame goes out.
Convection The water itself is moving — hot water rises because it’s lighter, cool water sinks to take its place. The water is the messenger carrying the heat around the pot. That’s convection — moving-fluid transfer.
Think Why does the soup taste hotter near the top after a few seconds of boiling?
Module 3Radiation — Sunlight at the Window
No touch, no fluid — heat as a wave that crosses empty space.

Sun strength 100%
Radiation No touch. No moving water. The Sun’s heat travels through empty space as invisible waves, then turns back into warmth when it hits something. That’s radiation — wave-based transfer that works through anything, even vacuum.
Think On a cold winter day, why does the spot by the window feel warm even when the heater is off?
